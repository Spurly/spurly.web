Use these after someone accepts your request, or after a message that got no reply. Replace everything in [brackets]. Keep each one short enough to read on a phone without scrolling. The sequence logic behind them is in [how to write a follow-up sequence](/blog/linkedin-follow-up-message-sequence).

## 1. First message after they accept

> Thanks for connecting, [First name]. I help [role] with [problem]. Curious how you handle [specific thing] at [Company] today?

## 2. Gentle nudge, three days later

> Hi [First name], bumping this in case it got buried. No worries if it isn't a priority right now.

## 3. Value add

> [First name], thought of you when I saw this on [topic]: [link]. It covers [one-line takeaway]. Hope it's useful.

## 4. Question-led follow-up

> Quick question, [First name]: when your team does [task], what's the part that takes the most time? Asking because we've been working on exactly that.

## 5. Light proof point

> Hi [First name], we recently helped [type of company] cut [thing] by [honest number or result]. If [problem] is on your radar, happy to share how.

## 6. Offer a smaller step

> No need for a call. If it's easier, I can send a two-line summary of what we do and you can tell me whether it's relevant.

## 7. Polite close

> I'll stop here so I don't clutter your inbox, [First name]. If [topic] becomes a priority, I'm easy to find. Wishing you a good quarter.

## 8. Re-engage after a few weeks

> Hi [First name], it's been a while. Saw [recent news or post from them] and thought it was worth a note. How's [project] going?

## What to avoid

Don't paste the same text in every step, and don't pretend a follow-up is a reply ("Re: our chat"). Do not keep following up after a clear no.
