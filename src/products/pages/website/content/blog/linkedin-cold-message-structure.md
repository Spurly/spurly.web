**Short answer:** a cold message that gets answered has four parts: a hook about them, a reason you are writing, one piece of value and a single easy ask. Keep it to three to five sentences and leave the pitch for later.

## The structure

1. **Hook.** Something true and specific about them: a post, a project, a role change.
2. **Reason.** Why you are writing to them in particular.
3. **Value.** What is in it for them: an idea, a resource, an intro or a relevant result you have seen.
4. **Ask.** One small, low-effort question, ideally answerable in a line.

## Example: sales

> Hi Priya, saw your post on hiring engineers in a tight market. I work with fintech teams on shortening that process. One idea that helped a few teams: [one concrete tactic]. Would it be useful if I sent over a short checklist?

## Example: recruiting

> Hi Sam, your work on [project] caught my eye. I'm hiring a [role] on a team doing similar things, and the scope is [one concrete detail]. Open to a 15-minute chat, or is the timing wrong?

## Example: job search

> Hi Dana, I'm a [role] who has followed your team's work on [topic]. I noticed you're hiring for [role]. Could I ask what the team values most in that hire?

## Why it works

- The first line is about them, so it reads as written for one person.
- The value comes before the ask.
- The ask is small, so saying yes costs little.
- Giving an easy "no" or "wrong timing" makes honest replies more likely.

## Mistakes

- Long paragraphs that explain your whole company.
- Generic compliments.
- Asking for 30 minutes before any trust exists.
- Sending the same text to everyone; see [personalising at scale](/blog/personalize-linkedin-connection-requests).
