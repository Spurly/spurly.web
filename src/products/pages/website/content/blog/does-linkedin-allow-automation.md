**Short answer:** no. LinkedIn's User Agreement and its help pages prohibit bots and other third-party software that automate activity on the site. Many people use automation tools anyway, and LinkedIn enforces its rules unevenly, but using one means accepting that LinkedIn can restrict your account.

## What LinkedIn's pages say

Two documents matter. The wording below is a paraphrase, cross-checked against other sources. LinkedIn can change its pages, so read the originals for exact text.

- **User Agreement, section 8.2.** The list of things members must not do includes using bots or other automated methods to access the service or send messages, and using scripts, crawlers or browser plug-ins to scrape or copy profiles and other data.
- **Help page: "Prohibited software and extensions".** It says LinkedIn does not permit third-party software, including bots, crawlers, browser plug-ins and extensions, that scrapes, modifies the appearance of or automates activity on LinkedIn. It says LinkedIn may restrict accounts that use it.

Gatling's 2026 summary of LinkedIn's guidance (19 August 2026) adds that accounts may be restricted or shut down and that tools may stop working without notice.

## What this means in practice

- The rule is about the activity, not the type of tool. A cloud tool and a browser extension are both third-party software acting for you.
- Enforcement is at LinkedIn's discretion. LinkedIn does not publish thresholds, so a lighter touch lowers risk without making automation compliant. Expandi puts it this way: staying below a chosen volume does not make prohibited activity compliant.
- A vendor cannot grant you permission. Only LinkedIn can.

## What is allowed

Using LinkedIn yourself, using its own paid products within their terms, and manual outreach. Features such as scheduling posts have the same ambiguity: LinkedIn's own tools may schedule, and a third-party scheduler is third-party software. Read the originals if you rely on this.

## Why tools exist anyway

Outreach is repetitive: finding people, sending notes, following up and tracking replies. Many sales, recruiting and founder workflows use tools for it, and vendors argue that modest, human-paced automation resembles normal use. LinkedIn's published position does not accept that argument, and enforcement has reached vendors themselves; HeyReach says LinkedIn removed its company page on 25 March 2026. The practical stance is to treat the risk as real, keep activity modest and decide whether the benefit is worth it for your profile.

## Where Spurly stands

Spurly automates actions on your LinkedIn account. That is against LinkedIn's rules, and we say so on our [safety page](/product/safety) rather than hiding it. We design limits to keep activity modest and we stop when someone replies, but we cannot promise your account will never be restricted. If that risk is unacceptable for your profile, do not use any automation tool, ours included.
