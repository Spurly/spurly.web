**Short answer:** LinkedIn does not document a warm-up period, so nobody can prove exactly how long one should be. But a gradual start costs you very little and a sudden jump in activity is one of the patterns vendors say gets noticed, so building up over one to two weeks is the cautious choice.

## What warm-up means

Warm-up is starting at a fraction of your intended volume and raising it step by step. Some tools do it for you. Expandi describes a model that begins at 5 actions a day and reaches 21 over two weeks (its page of 28 September 2026). Others leave it to you.

## What is known and what is not

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn publishes a warm-up schedule | False; none is documented |
| A sudden jump in activity looks unusual | Plausible and widely reported by vendors, not documented by LinkedIn |
| New accounts tolerate less | Zeliq says so; unproven by LinkedIn |
| A specific ramp, such as +5 a day, is optimal | No evidence; each vendor picks its own |

So warm-up is a precaution, not a rule. Zeliq goes further and says extensions on accounts under three months old almost guarantee a restriction; that is one vendor's view and we do not repeat it as fact.

## A simple ramp you can follow

Spurly's limits apply from day one and do not change by themselves, so you set the pace by how much you enroll in a [campaign](/product/campaigns).

1. **Days 1 to 3.** Enroll a small audience, around 10 to 15 people a day. Check that the messages read well and that people accept.
2. **Days 4 to 7.** Raise to about 20 a day if your acceptance rate is healthy.
3. **Week 2.** Move toward 25 to 30 a day.
4. **Week 3 onward.** Only go higher if acceptance stays good, and stay below the [weekly limit](/blog/linkedin-weekly-invitation-limit) and the [daily limits](/blog/linkedin-automation-limits-per-day).

These numbers are our suggestion, not a LinkedIn requirement, and they are not a guarantee. If LinkedIn shows a warning at any point, stop and read [what to do](/blog/linkedin-account-restricted-what-to-do).

## What to watch during warm-up

- **Acceptance rate.** If fewer than about one in four accept, fix the audience or note before raising volume. This threshold is our rule of thumb, not LinkedIn's.
- **Warnings.** Any prompt to verify identity or a limit message means stop and step back.
- **Replies.** Answer them yourself from the inbox so the account shows normal two-way activity.

## What also helps

Use your account normally on LinkedIn: log in, reply to messages, react to posts. An account that only ever sends invitations looks different from one that is used. Complete the profile with a real photo and history.
