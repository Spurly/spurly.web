**Short answer:** LinkedIn does not publish a fixed weekly limit for connection requests. Vendors that write about it report somewhere between about 100 and about 200 invitations per week, and LinkedIn can apply a lower limit to a specific account. The safe way to plan is to treat the limit as unknown, start well below the vendor figures and watch for LinkedIn telling you that you have reached it.

## What is known

When you hit the limit, LinkedIn shows a message that you have reached the weekly invitation limit and cannot send more until it resets. That message is how most people learn the limit exists.

Beyond that, LinkedIn's help centre does not give a number. Expandi's page on connection limits (published 22 September 2026) says plainly that LinkedIn does not publish a fixed numeric weekly or daily allowance, and that it applies to free, Premium and Sales Navigator accounts alike. Expandi recommends staying within about 100 invitations a week, and calls that a planning figure and not a LinkedIn entitlement.

Waalaxy's page (updated 11 September 2026) says LinkedIn reduced the number of possible invitations to about 200 per week, from more than 500 before. Zeliq's 2026 guide gives 100 to 200 a week for connection requests. All three are vendors writing about LinkedIn, not LinkedIn.

## Why the number differs between accounts

Vendors agree on one thing: the allowance is not the same for everyone. Zeliq says older accounts with a high acceptance rate tolerate more than new ones. Expandi says a stronger Social Selling Index brings no published promise of more invitations. In practice, a new account, a low acceptance rate or a recent burst of activity are reasons to stay lower.

## What to do when LinkedIn says you have reached the limit

1. Stop sending invitations from every tool, not only the one you were using. The allowance is shared.
2. Wait for it to roll over. LinkedIn does not document the exact window, so give it several days if the message persists.
3. Use the time to improve the audience and the note, and to withdraw old pending invitations.
4. When you resume, return to a lower daily rate than the one that hit the limit.

Waalaxy and others describe tricks that save invitations, such as finding email addresses first and writing by email instead. Those change how you reach people, not what LinkedIn allows, and we do not treat them as ways around the limit.

## How Spurly handles it

Spurly counts every connection request it sends over a rolling seven days and shares that count between the app and the Chrome extension, because LinkedIn counts them together. When the weekly budget is spent, [campaigns](/product/campaigns) stop sending connection requests and resume as the window moves on. You are notified when you are running low.

Within a day, connection requests are limited to 40 a day and 8 an hour by default, with at least two minutes between them. See [daily limits per action](/blog/linkedin-automation-limits-per-day). The per-day cap exists so a week's budget is spread out, not spent in one burst.

## How to plan your week

1. Pick a weekly number below the lowest vendor figure, for example under 100, for the first two weeks.
2. Spread it over the days you work. Avoid a burst on Monday.
3. Keep some allowance for people you meet by hand. Invitations you send from LinkedIn itself count against the same limit.
4. Send to people likely to accept. Ignored requests do not use less allowance, and a low acceptance rate is a risk of its own; see [what gets accounts restricted](/blog/what-gets-linkedin-accounts-restricted).
5. If LinkedIn tells you the limit is reached, stop sending. Do not try to work around it.
