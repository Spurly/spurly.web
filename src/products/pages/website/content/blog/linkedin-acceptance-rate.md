**Short answer:** there is no official benchmark for a good LinkedIn acceptance rate. Measure your own, compare it against your past results and treat a sharp drop as a signal to fix your list or note. As a floor, one vendor guide says an acceptance rate below about 15% can look like spam to LinkedIn.

## How to calculate it

Acceptance rate = accepted requests ÷ requests sent × 100. Count requests sent in a period, wait a week or two for people to respond and then count how many accepted. Pending invitations count as not yet accepted.

Example: 80 sent, 40 accepted, 20 still pending, 20 withdrawn or ignored. The rate is 40 ÷ 80 = 50% so far, and it may rise as the pending ones resolve.

## What moves it

| Lever | Effect |
|---|---|
| Audience relevance | The biggest one. People accept people they can place. |
| A real reason in the note | Gives a reason to say yes. |
| Your profile | A clear, credible profile helps people decide. |
| Shared context | A mutual connection or group lifts acceptance. |
| Volume and timing | Sending to too many in a short time lowers quality of targeting. |

## Why it matters beyond results

Tool vendors link a low rate to restrictions: ignored requests and "I don't know this person" reports are signals that outreach is unwanted. LinkedIn does not publish its rules for this, so we treat it as a precaution. See [what gets accounts restricted](/blog/what-gets-linkedin-accounts-restricted).

## How to improve it

1. Narrow the audience before you change the message.
2. Add one real detail per person.
3. Shorten the note.
4. Withdraw old pending invitations so they do not pile up.
5. Test one change at a time on batches of at least 30 to 50 people.

Spurly detects acceptances automatically for your connections and lets you withdraw stale invitations, so the numbers are easy to read. Insights charts are not available yet.
