**Short answer:** stop all automation immediately, follow the steps LinkedIn shows you (usually identity verification), use LinkedIn's appeal form if you are blocked, and do not create a second account. Some restrictions lift in days, others take months, and some are permanent.

This is not legal or account-recovery advice, and no one outside LinkedIn can promise a result.

## Step by step

1. **Stop everything.** Pause campaigns and sequences in every tool, including Spurly. Do not keep sending from other tools or from LinkedIn itself.
2. **Read what LinkedIn says.** The message tells you which restriction applies and what it asks for. Take a screenshot.
3. **Verify your identity if asked.** Datablist describes identity verification through a government ID check as one official route. Use the real details on the profile.
4. **Appeal.** Datablist points to LinkedIn's dedicated appeal form at linkedin.com/help/linkedin/ask/ts-f-appeal and says the safety team usually responds within a week or two.
5. **Wait.** Waalaxy says a restriction can last from a few days to several months, and that if nothing happens after two weeks there may be little more you can do.
6. **Do not work around it.** A second account, a VPN or a different tool on the same account can be treated as another violation.

## Kinds of restriction

Datablist describes two broad outcomes. A temporary or indefinite restriction limits some or all features, and the account is not always gone for good. A permanent restriction means the account and its features can no longer be used and the profile is no longer findable. Which you get depends on the violation and on whether it is a repeat.

## What to avoid

- Contacting LinkedIn repeatedly with the same appeal.
- Sending more automated activity from the account while a review is open.
- Telling the platform the account was hacked if it was not.
- Buying a "restriction removal" service. Nobody outside LinkedIn can lift it.

## When you get back in

- Resume without automation first and use LinkedIn normally for a while.
- If you return to automation, start at a lower volume than before and build up; see [warming up an account](/blog/linkedin-account-warm-up).
- Look at what likely caused it, using [what gets accounts restricted](/blog/what-gets-linkedin-accounts-restricted): volume, ignored requests, spam reports or a new account.
- Keep your list targeted so acceptance stays healthy.

## What Spurly does and does not do

If LinkedIn disconnects your account or several actions fail in a row, Spurly pauses the campaign rather than working through your audience, and you can see why in the campaign. It does not repeat a send that may already have gone through. It cannot lift a restriction or speed up LinkedIn's review, and it cannot guarantee that an account will not be restricted; see [how Spurly keeps your account safe](/product/safety).

If you have not been restricted, the practical prevention steps are in [is LinkedIn automation safe?](/blog/is-linkedin-automation-safe).
