**Short answer:** send a thank-you when they accept, a useful message a few days later and one or two short reminders a week or more apart, then stop. End the sequence the moment someone replies. The timings below are suggestions from practice, not LinkedIn rules.

## The four steps

| Step | When | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Thank-you | Right after they accept | Be a person, ask one easy question |
| 2. Useful message | 3 to 4 days later | Offer something relevant, not a pitch |
| 3. Short reminder | A week later | Make it easy to say yes or no |
| 4. Polite close | A week or more later | Leave the door open and stop |

### 1. The thank-you

> Thanks for connecting, [Name]. I reached out because [reason]. Curious, how are you handling [topic] at [company] these days?

One question, no link.

### 2. The useful message

> [Name], a quick thought after looking at [company]: [one relevant observation or resource]. If it is useful, I can share how others handle it.

Offer something they can use even if they never reply.

### 3. The reminder

> Hi [Name], floating this back up in case it got buried. Worth a short chat, or is [topic] not a priority right now?

Giving them an easy no raises honest replies.

### 4. The close

> I'll leave it here so I don't clutter your inbox. If [topic] becomes relevant, I'm happy to help. Wishing you a good quarter.

## Rules that keep it respectful

- **Stop on reply.** Never send a scripted follow-up into a live conversation. Spurly stops a sequence for a person who replies.
- **Stop on no.** Thank them and stop.
- **Vary the wording.** Do not send three messages that only say "following up".
- **Keep each one short.** Three or four sentences at most.
- **Use real details.** A name and company filled in correctly is the minimum.

## Doing it by hand or with a tool

By hand, set reminders and keep a note of where each person is. With a tool, build the steps once and let it handle the timing.
