**Short answer:** a founder can run LinkedIn outreach without an SDR by defining one sharp ICP, building a list from a search, letting AI draft one template that you approve, and running it as a campaign with follow-ups from one inbox. A tool like Spurly sends it from the cloud, so it takes minutes a day instead of a hire.

Early on, most founders *are* the sales team. You don't have budget for an SDR, and a full CRM is overkill when you're still figuring out who your buyer even is. The good news: LinkedIn plus a lightweight workflow is enough to keep a steady pipeline — and with AI drafting the messages and a cloud tool sending them, it takes minutes a day instead of a hire.

## Step 1 — Define one sharp ICP

Don't try to reach "everyone who might buy." Pick one ideal customer profile you can describe in a sentence: a title, a company type, and a size. The narrower your list, the more relevant your outreach, and the higher your reply rate.

## Step 2 — Build the list from a search

Run a LinkedIn or Sales Navigator search that matches your ICP. This is your raw pipeline. Instead of copying names into a spreadsheet by hand, build the audience inside [Spurly](/): filtered people search, people at target companies, authors of relevant posts, or any LinkedIn URL you paste.

## Step 3 — Personalize, then reach out

Let the AI draft one template with variables, edit it to sound like you, and let it adapt per person. Relevance is what gets replies — we break the mechanics down in [how to personalize LinkedIn connection requests at scale](/blog/personalize-linkedin-connection-requests). Aim for a steady daily cadence rather than one big burst. Spurly runs the campaign from the cloud at a safe pace, and stops following up the moment someone replies.

## Step 4 — Follow up and track

Most replies come from the follow-up, not the first touch. Set up a sequence (invite, wait for the accept, message, follow up) and keep your replies in one inbox, so you always know who has been contacted, who replied, and who needs a nudge. That organization *is* your CRM at this stage.
